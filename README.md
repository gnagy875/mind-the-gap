# Hangközelítő / Mind the Gap 🎵

**Hangközelítő** (English name: **Mind the Gap**) is a small web app for learning musical intervals. An interval is the distance between two notes — for example, C to G is a perfect fifth.

## What can you do with it?

The app has three modes. You can switch between them with the tabs at the top.

### 🎼 By notation

The app shows you two note names (for example, G♯ → A♯). You have to choose the correct interval from the answer buttons. The second note is always the higher one. After each answer, the app tells you if you were right, and explains the result (letter steps and semitones).

### 🎧 By ear

The app hides the note names and plays two tones instead. You listen, then choose the interval you heard. The answer buttons only become active after your first listen, and you can replay the notes as many times as you want. After you answer, the app reveals the notes, so you can listen again and learn.

### 🎹 Practice

This mode has no quiz and no score. You pick a starting note from a list, then press any interval button. The app builds the second note for you and plays both tones. You can also click each note card to hear that note alone. This is a free space to train your ear before you take the quiz.

## Two languages

You can switch between Hungarian and English with the flag buttons in the top right corner. This changes more than just the texts:

- **Hungarian** uses the Hungarian/German note names: C, D, E, F, G, A, **H**. The lowered H is called **B**.
- **English** uses the international names: C, D, E, F, G, A, **B**. The lowered B is written **B♭**.
- The interval names change too: *tiszta kvint* becomes *perfect fifth*, *nagy szekund* becomes *major second*, and so on.

Hungarian is the default language.

## A few music theory rules inside the app

- The name of an interval depends on **two things**: the distance between the letter names, and the number of semitones. That is why G♯ → A♯ is a *major second* (2 semitones), but G♯ → A is a *minor second* (1 semitone), and G → G♯ is an *augmented unison*.
- Sharps (♯) and flats (♭) are **never mixed** in one question. A question uses either the sharp set of notes or the flat set.
- In the "By ear" mode, the spelling does not matter — G♯ and A♭ sound the same. There, only the number of semitones counts, and the octave is also part of the answers.
